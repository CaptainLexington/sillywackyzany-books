While he was still entertaining a career in medicine, Michael Crichton wrote under three pseudonyms - John Lange, Jeffery Hudson, and "Michael Douglas". This Jeffrey Hudson is probably the cheapest of any of these books to acquire, so I have no temptation not to keep it. The seed is planted! Of course, this July, 1969 Signet edition is not the first edition of *A Case of Need*, but it is the first *paperback*. Printed a year after the hardcover release, and just two months after *The Andromeda Strain* was released, Crichton was now much more famous under his own name, and retired his pseudonyms in a few years.

It's astonishing the breadth of genres that can be reduced to bare-shouldered women. *Tales* is usually considered literary fiction; *Joe and Jennie* and *(The Powerful Story of)* *Jeanne* are just sex stories; *The Beach House* is a mainstream, if not particularly literary, novel; *Don't Push Me Around* and *Knee-Deep in Death* are what today we would call thrillers; *The Long Wait* is Mickey Spillane's particular brand of hard-boiled detective, and *Friend or Foe?* is a spy story. Pretty much any story that was or could be marketed at (presumed-straight) men could be sold with skin.
